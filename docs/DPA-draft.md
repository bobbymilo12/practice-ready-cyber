## 1. Subject matter and duration
The Processor provides online training for the Controller's staff. This agreement runs for as long
as the Processor holds personal data for the Controller.

## 2. Nature, purpose and data
- **Purpose:** hosting accounts, assigning and recording staff training, and showing results to the
  Controller's managers.
- **Data subjects:** the Controller's managers and staff.
- **Personal data:** name, work email, job role, app role, assigned modules, answers, scores,
  confidence ratings, completion dates, manager action log entries (who did what, and when).
- **Not intended:** patient data and special category data. The Controller must not enter either
  into training content, local procedures or free-text fields.

## 3. Processor obligations (UK GDPR Art. 28(3))
1. Process personal data only on the Controller's documented instructions, including for transfers,
   unless law requires otherwise (the Processor will say so first, where lawful).
2. Make sure everyone with access is bound by confidentiality.
3. Apply appropriate security (Art. 32): encryption in transit, role- and practice-based access
   control, separation of each practice's data, and no passwords stored in readable form.
4. Use only the sub-processors in section 5, and tell the Controller of any change at least
   [30] days ahead. The Controller may object on reasonable grounds.
5. Help the Controller answer data-subject requests (access, correction, erasure, restriction),
   within [5] working days of a request. The product lets managers deactivate and permanently
   erase a person.
6. Tell the Controller of a personal data breach without undue delay and within **24 hours** of
   becoming aware, with the facts needed for the Controller's own ICO notification.
7. Help with security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments and prior
   consultation, taking into account the information available to the Processor.
8. At the end of the service, delete all personal data within [90] days unless the Controller asks
   for it to be returned first, and unless law requires it to be kept.
9. Provide the information needed to show compliance and allow audits by the Controller or its
   auditor, on [30] days' notice, no more than once a year unless there has been a breach.
10. Tell the Controller if an instruction appears to break data protection law.

## 4. Controller obligations
The Controller is responsible for having a lawful basis for the processing, telling its staff what
data is used and that managers can see their results, and not entering patient data.

The Processor stays responsible for its sub-processors and flows the same obligations down to them.

## 6. International transfers
Personal data is stored in the UK and the EEA. Where a sub-processor or its parent could access it
from elsewhere (for example the United States), the Processor relies on UK adequacy regulations,
the UK International Data Transfer Agreement, or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual
Clauses.
